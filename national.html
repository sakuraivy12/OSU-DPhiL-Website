About Us

Our History

Discover the story behind the creation of Delta Phi Lambda, an Asian-interest sorority built on sisterhood, cultural awareness, and empowerment. From overcoming challenges at the University of Georgia to forming a lasting legacy, explore how our founders turned their vision into a thriving organization.

1. Struggling for Connection

The Challenge of Belonging

When Ms. Anh Ngoc Nguyen began her studies at the University of Georgia, she struggled to find peers who understood her Asian background. In a predominantly white school of 30,000 students, minorities, especially Asians, were underrepresented. Worse still, there was a lack of unity among the few Asian students on campus.

2. A Vision for Sisterhood

The Idea takes Shape

In the winter of 1998, Ms. Nguyen and Ms. Theresa Sung discussed creating an Asian-interest sorority to promote cultural awareness and break down stereotypes about Asians. With support from friends and family, Ms. Nguyen set out to find women who shared her belief in fostering unity through sisterhood.

3. The Need for Change

Cultural Unity through Sisterhood

The founders believed that existing Asian student clubs, often divided by ethnicity, couldn’t serve the entire community effectively. These clubs lacked cohesion, with fluctuating membership and leadership turnover. The founders felt a sorority would be a better way to promote Asian cultures and provide a sense of belonging, especially for students who were homesick or seeking a supportive, family-like environment.

4. Delta Phi Lambda is Founded

A Mission Realized

On December 5, 1998, the founding class—Anh Ngoc Nguyen, Theresa Sung, Sarah Chong Mi Cho, Carmela de Guzman, Yvonne Minh Ta, Linh Khanh Do, and Rebecca Kim Stephenson—officially created Delta Phi Lambda. Despite setbacks, opposition, and challenges, they remained committed to their mission of building an organization that would nurture, empower, and support its members while promoting Asian awareness on campus.
